    A day in the life

    What a session looks like, start to finish

    Every session is one hour. The same three parts, every week, so kids always know what comes next.

    1. 3:15Pick-up from class. Kids walk to the room together. Parents don't have to leave work early. [CONFIRM: school's dismissal routine]
    2. 3:20Story and one fact. The facilitator reads the week's fact out loud. Kids pick their storyline, from three options or their own.
    3. 3:30Build. Kids paint, decorate and stick together that week's pieces. Juniors get pre-cut parts. Seniors trace and cut some of their own.
    4. 3:55Test and show. Kids use the piece in their story and show a partner what it does.
    5. 4:05Pack-up and pick-up. Work goes into the kit. Kids leave with what they made that day.

    Example, from a Week 4 Mars session: a child builds a rover arm with a claw, then uses it to pick up a sample box in the story. Their partner says what the arm reaches.

    What kids learn

    Real facts, built into the story

    Every theme teaches real science and history, checked against sources. Facts are paced for age: Juniors (5–7) learn two or three a week. Seniors (8–11) learn about eight, and use three in their story.

    Example, Mars week 1: Olympus Mons is about two and a half times the height of Mount Everest, and it's the largest volcano in the solar system.

    Junior (5–7)

    Pre-cut pieces to paint, decorate and stick together. The facilitator helps with the joins. One paper-cup build each session in some weeks.

    Senior (8–11)

    Trace and cut some parts, build moving linkages on split pins, and write a short storyline with a beginning, a problem and an ending.

    Pick a world

    Six weeks. One story. Four worlds.

    Each block runs one world for all six weeks. Week 1 builds the backdrop. Weeks 2 to 5 add colour, gear and machines. Week 6 is the show for parents.

    Two blocks, two days, two worlds

    A school can host more than one block. Each block runs on its own day with its own world, so the same kids can't get bored, and a school doesn't need more than one room free at a time.
